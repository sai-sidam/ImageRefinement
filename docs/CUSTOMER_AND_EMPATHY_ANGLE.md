# June & Ember — Customer understanding & empathy angle

**Purpose:** No gap between what we make and what the customer wants. Every post, caption, image, and strategy decision must be grounded in **who she is**, **what she feels**, and **what she wants** — not only in what we want to say. Use this doc so we never leave the customer angle unturned.

**Research basis:** Fashion and occasion-wear psychology, Instagram audience behavior, empathy-driven DTC branding, “nothing to wear” and style anxiety, trust and connection on social, inclusive messaging. Updated with web research so we don’t rely on assumptions.

---

## 1. Who she is (customer)

- **Occasion-wear shopper:** She buys for events and moments — weddings, parties, dinners, “that one night,” holidays, milestones. She’s not buying for the closet in the abstract; she’s buying for **a moment**.
- **Values:** She cares about how she **feels** (confident, put-together, like herself) as much as how she looks. Comfort and fit that work with her body matter; she doesn’t want to be constantly adjusting or second-guessing.
- **Identity:** She uses clothes to express who she is and how she wants to be seen. Occasion wear is part of that — “this is the version of me I want to show for this moment.”
- **Instagram:** She discovers brands and looks on Instagram. She wants **trust** (real product, real look), **relatability** (attainable, not unapproachable), and **connection** (brands that feel like they get her), not just pretty pictures.

---

## 2. What she feels (emotional drivers)

From research and fashion/psychology sources:

| Feeling | What it means for June & Ember |
|--------|--------------------------------|
| **Joy, anticipation** | Excitement for the event or the moment; she wants the dress to be part of that excitement, not a source of stress. |
| **Anxiety** | “Nothing to wear,” style anxiety, decision paralysis. Nearly 1 in 3 women experience style anxiety when getting dressed. She wants options that **reduce** that anxiety — one great piece, clear occasion, “this is the one.” |
| **Desire for confidence** | She wants to feel good in her skin. Research links clothing that fits and flatters to higher confidence (e.g. comfort and fit improving how she feels). We speak to “feel beautiful,” “feel put-together,” “feel like yourself.” |
| **Wanting to be seen** | She wants to feel **understood** — that the brand gets her life, her moments, her body, her style. Inclusive, “we see you” messaging builds loyalty. |
| **Trust** | She’s wary of buying online (returns, color/fit mismatch). She wants a brand that feels **transparent**, **consistent**, and **real** — not just aspirational fluff. |

So: we’re not selling “a dress.” We’re speaking to **moments that matter**, **confidence**, **ease** (no “nothing to wear” panic), and **being seen**.

---

## 3. Pain points we address

- **“I have nothing to wear”** — Overstuffed closet, wrong occasion, or nothing that feels right. We offer **clear occasion pieces** and messaging that says “this one’s for that moment.”
- **Fit and comfort anxiety** — Will it look good on me? Will I feel good? We emphasize **flattering silhouettes**, **quality fabric**, and copy that focuses on “feels as good as it looks” and “dress for your body.”
- **Decision overload** — Too many options, can’t choose. We keep the feed and messaging **focused**: hero pieces, clear occasions, one strong CTA.
- **Distrust of online shopping** — “Will it look like the photo?” We keep **product color and design accurate**, visuals **coherent and real** (no obvious AI), and tone **honest and welcoming**.

---

## 4. What she wants from us on Instagram

From Instagram/fashion research:

- **Trust over follower count** — She values **genuine connection**, transparency, and consistency more than big numbers. Our first post and every post should build trust.
- **Relatability** — “Aspirational but achievable.” She wants to feel the brand is **for her** — luxurious yet approachable, not elite or unattainable.
- **Purpose and “who you help”** — She wants to know **what we’re for** and **who we serve**. First post and bio should make that clear: occasion wear, moments that matter, women who want to feel elegant and confident.
- **Authenticity** — Real-looking imagery, real voice. No fake perfection; no “post and hope.” Relationship over one-off viral hit.

So: every caption, image choice, and strategy decision should pass the test: *Does this help her trust us, feel seen, and understand that we’re here for her moments?*

---

## 5. The empathy angle (one statement)

**We understand:** You have moments that matter — events, dinners, celebrations, “that one night.” You want to feel **confident**, **put-together**, and **like yourself**, not stressed by “nothing to wear” or by clothes that don’t feel right. You want occasion wear that’s **elegant and effortless**, that works with your life and your body, and that you can trust when you shop online.

**We’re here for:** Those moments. We make pieces you’ll reach for when it matters — so you can show up feeling beautiful, not anxious.

Use this to check every piece of content: Does it **reflect** this understanding (empathy) or does it only **describe** the product (no empathy)?

---

## 6. How this shapes what we do

| What we do | Empathy / customer angle |
|------------|---------------------------|
| **First post** | Welcome her (“So glad you’re here”), state what we’re for (“moments that matter,” “elegant, effortless”), and invite her in (tap to shop). Not just “we’re live” — we’re **here for you**. |
| **Captions** | Lead with **her** when possible: “Save this for your next ‘I have nothing to wear’ moment,” “The kind of piece you reach for when you want to feel put-together in 10 minutes,” “Feels as good as it looks.” |
| **Visuals** | Model and setting that feel **attainable** and **real** (natural, confident, any look that fits the brand) so she can see herself in the moment, not an unattainable fantasy. |
| **Strategy** | Trust first (real imagery, consistent voice); relatability (aspirational but achievable); clarity on who we help (occasion wear, confidence, ease). |
| **Tone** | Warm, welcoming, confident but not cold or exclusive. “So glad you’re here” not “Check out our new drop.” |

---

## 7. Checklist before we ship anything

Before we lock a post, caption, prompt, or strategy:

- [ ] **Customer:** Have we thought about what **she** wants or feels, not only what we want to say?
- [ ] **Empathy:** Does the message **reflect** that we understand her (moments, confidence, ease, trust)?
- [ ] **Trust:** Does the image and copy feel **real** and **consistent** with our brand (no fake, no gap)?
- [ ] **Relatability:** Is this **aspirational but achievable** — would she feel “this is for me”?
- [ ] **Clarity:** Is it clear **who we help** and **what we’re for** (occasion wear, moments that matter)?

If any box is unchecked, we’re leaving a gap. Fix it before we call it done.
