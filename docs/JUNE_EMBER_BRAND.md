## Mood Board Summary (Visual Foundation)

The June & Ember mood board sets the visual foundation: **elegant, feminine, aspirational** — like a curated scrapbook with a warm, luxurious base.

- **Base:** Textured warm neutrals (beige, light brown, soft pinks), gold accents, pearl/ribbon touches. Feels inviting and elevated.
- **Product:** Dresses as hero — mini to maxi, form-fitting silhouettes, feminine details (deep V, halter, one-shoulder, wrap, ruching, slits). Silky/satin, knit, denim. Jewel tones (burgundy, magenta, teal, shimmery purple) and crisp white, denim blue, olive, warm brown.
- **Settings:** Light architecture, archways, stone, greenery; Mediterranean/resort feel. Elegant interiors (e.g. ornate gold, clean bedroom). Well-lit, clean, aspirational — no cluttered or overly casual.
- **Posing & vibe:** Confident, graceful, natural. Approachable but polished. Minimalist jewelry, small chic bags, straw hats with earthy looks; wine/cocktail for occasion. Content should feel **luxurious yet approachable**.

## Primary Lighting

- **Soft, flattering, warm** — Natural light preferred; bright but diffused (e.g. by architecture, greenery, or window). Golden-hour warmth or clean daylight. No harsh midday or cold blue hour. *Your tweak:* Warm, soft, flattering light with gentle diffusion and natural skin tones; avoid cold/blue casts; allow golden hour and clean daylight as primary looks.

## Content Pillars & Rotation

1. **Product hero** — Clean or soft warm-background dress shot (store + feed); product centered.
2. **Lifestyle** — Dress in aspirational context: light architecture, archways, greenery, or elegant interior; confident, graceful pose.
3. **Flat-lay / detail** — Fabric, texture, or curated flat-lay (warm neutrals, minimal props) for grid rhythm.
4. **Occasion / styling** — “How to wear” or event-ready look; optional subtle accessories (minimal jewelry, small bag, straw hat with earthy looks).

**Face vs. no-face (expert rule):** First image in a post/carousel = **with face** (hero, connection). Second image onward = **no face** (detail, focus on garment). Single image = with face. The tool applies this automatically for `--style=juneember`. See [SOCIAL_MEDIA_EXPERT.md](SOCIAL_MEDIA_EXPERT.md).

**Settings to use:** Mediterranean/resort feel (stone, arches, plants), elegant interiors (clean, soft light, optional gold accents), or simple elevated neutrals. **Avoid:** cluttered backgrounds, overly casual or cold environments.

**Grid pattern (starter):** Product → Lifestyle → Product (or Hero with face → Detail no face). Keep a repeating rhythm.

*Your tweak:* Maintain a consistent rhythm: bold hero (with face) → detail (no face) → lifestyle, so the grid alternates connection and product focus.

## Technical (Instagram)

- **Aspect ratio:** **4:5** (1080 × 1350 px) for feed.
- **Safe zone:** Key subject (product, face) in the **center 70%**; avoid important detail in top/bottom 15% and far edges (profile grid crops to 3:4).
- **Width:** 1080 px.

---

## Edit Style

- **Warm, soft, slightly lifted** — Warmth +10–15, shadows lifted, soft contrast. Muted beige/brown/gold undertones so the feed feels like the mood board. No cold blues or harsh contrast unless it’s a deliberate one-off.
- Filter/Preset: 'Luxe Warmth' - A custom preset characterized by subtle warmth, enhanced skin tones, a soft diffusion in highlights, and rich, deep shadows, ensuring colors maintain their vibrancy without oversaturation.

*Your tweak:* Keep skin tones warm and natural; preserve fabric texture; avoid heavy sharpening and avoid plastic skin.

## Do Not

- **Change the product’s color** — Refined images must keep the garment’s exact color, pattern, and fabric. Only background/lighting/setting may change.
- Mix lighting (e.g. cold and warm in the same 9-post block).
- Use cluttered, overly casual, or cold/clinical backgrounds.
- Put key subject (product, face) at the very edges — keep in center safe zone.
- Use random accent colors; stick to warm neutrals + jewel/classic palette from the mood board.
- Overpower the dress with busy props or heavy accessories (keep jewelry and props minimal and elegant).
- Post without checking how the tile looks next to the last 2–3 posts.

*Your add:* Do not use imagery that feels exclusive in a negative way; keep luxury aspirational but still welcoming and relatable.

## Visual System (Expert Guide)

This section is the **single source of truth** for building and maintaining a consistent visual system on Instagram. Use it when defining the look, planning content, and when using this tool so every asset fits the same system.

**Why it matters:** First impression in 2–3 seconds; consistent identity can deliver ~3.5× more brand visibility; cohesive feed reads as intentional; case studies show ~35% engagement lift with consistent warm-toned filters. The grid is your digital storefront.

**Four pillars of feed consistency** (lock at least 3): **Lighting** (one dominant style; avoid mixing midday, golden hour, blue hour randomly); **Color** (3–5 colors, same filter/preset); **Subject/content type** (content pillars + repeating pattern); **Location/setting** (consistent backgrounds). Mixed lighting is one of the biggest cohesion killers — pick one mood and stick to it.

**Color system:** Three layers — brand colors (2–3 primary), content colors (recurring in photos), post-to-post glue (one recurring hue/neutral). Use 3–5 consistent colors; include one neutral base; document hex codes. For this tool: describe color mood in the prompt.

**Lighting & mood:** Define one primary mood (e.g. soft golden hour, bright and airy). Fashion options: bright & airy, golden hour, studio/even, moody/dramatic. Use style presets and prompts that specify lighting so every generated image shares the same language.

**Content mix & rhythm:** Content pillars (3–5 types), rotate in a repeating pattern. Format mix: single images (clarity), carousels (often highest engagement), Reels (best for reach). Suggested balance: ~60% photos, ~30% carousels, ~10% Reels. Grid patterns: checkerboard, rows, diagonal, puzzle.

**Technical specs:** 4:5 portrait 1080×1350 px (default); width 1080 px. Profile grid crops to 3:4 — keep critical content in center 70–80%; leave top/bottom 10–15% clear; avoid key elements at far edges.

**Editing consistency:** One primary edit style; document filter or sliders. **Seasonal tweaks:** Autumn/Winter — richer jewel tones (emerald, sapphire, ruby, amethyst), warm earthy neutrals; warmer, softer, more dramatic lighting; luxurious interiors, cozy-luxe lounges; elegant layering, velvet/silk, fine jewelry. Spring/Summer — lighter pastels (blush, sky blue, mint, lavender), vibrant pops (coral, fuchsia, teal); bright natural daylight, golden hour; open-air (botanical gardens, seaside, rooftop); breathable fabrics, fluid silhouettes, minimal accessories.

**Checklist before publishing any feed image:** Aspect ratio 4:5; key subject in center safe zone; lighting matches mood; colors fit palette; style matches content pillar; edit matches preset; grid check vs last 2–3 posts; caption + hashtags ready.

**How this tool supports the system:** `--aspect=4:5`, `--style=juneember` (or clean, lifestyle, flatlay, etc.), custom prompts for lighting/color, product centered, same preset + aspect = repeatable look.

## Our Customer & Empathy Angle

**Purpose:** No gap between what we make and what the customer wants. Every post, caption, image, and strategy decision must be grounded in **who she is**, **what she feels**, and **what she wants**. Research basis: fashion and occasion-wear psychology, Instagram audience behavior, empathy-driven DTC branding, style anxiety, trust and connection on social.

**Who she is:** Occasion-wear shopper (events, moments, milestones). Cares how she feels (confident, put-together, like herself); comfort and fit matter. Uses clothes to express who she is. On Instagram she wants trust (real product, real look), relatability, and connection.

**What she feels:** Joy and anticipation for the moment; anxiety (“nothing to wear,” style anxiety — we reduce it); desire for confidence; wanting to be seen (understood); trust (transparent, consistent, real). We speak to moments that matter, confidence, ease, and being seen.

**Pain points we address:** “Nothing to wear” → clear occasion pieces; fit/comfort anxiety → flattering silhouettes, “feels as good as it looks”; decision overload → focused feed and messaging; distrust of online shopping → accurate product, coherent real visuals, honest tone.

**What she wants from us on Instagram:** Trust over follower count; relatability (aspirational but achievable); purpose and “who you help”; authenticity. Test: *Does this help her trust us, feel seen, and understand we’re here for her moments?*

**The empathy angle (one statement):** We understand you have moments that matter; you want to feel confident, put-together, and like yourself, not stressed by “nothing to wear.” We’re here for those moments — pieces you’ll reach for when it matters, so you can show up feeling beautiful, not anxious.

**How this shapes what we do:** First post = welcome her, state what we’re for, invite her in. Captions = lead with her when possible. Visuals = attainable and real. Strategy = trust first, relatability, clarity. Tone = warm, welcoming, confident but not exclusive.

**Checklist before we ship anything:** Customer angle considered? Empathy reflected? Trust (real, consistent)? Relatability (aspirational but achievable)? Clarity on who we help?
